An online degree from an Indian university is only as good as its approval. Here is how to check it yourself before you pay anyone, us included.

## 1. Check the university on the UGC-DEB website

The UGC's Distance Education Bureau (DEB) publishes the list of higher education institutions that are **entitled to offer online programs**. Open the DEB website (deb.ugc.ac.in), go to the list of recognised HEIs for online programs, and look for:

- the **university's name**, exactly as it appears on the degree,
- the **academic session** you plan to join, and
- the **specific program** (for example "MBA" or "MCA"), because entitlement is given program by program.

If the program isn't listed for your session, ask the university for its approval letter before you apply.

## 2. Look for the university's mandatory disclosures

Universities offering online programs must publish their approvals, regulations and program details on their website, usually on a page called "Regulations and Approvals" or "Mandatory Disclosures". Look for UGC entitlement letters, the NAAC accreditation certificate, and AICTE approval for MBA and MCA where it applies.

## 3. Understand what "equivalent" means

Under the UGC (Open and Distance Learning Programmes and Online Programmes) Regulations, 2020, a degree earned online from an entitled university is treated as **equivalent to the corresponding degree earned in regular, on-campus mode**. This matters for government jobs, promotions and further study in India.

## 4. Get your ABC ID ready

Universities now ask for a 12-digit **Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) ID**. You can create one free at abc.gov.in through DigiLocker. Your credits are recorded against it.

## 5. Watch for red flags

- The fee is paid to an individual or agency account instead of the university.
- You are promised a degree in less than the minimum duration.
- You are told there are "no exams".
- The degree name doesn't match the name on the UGC-DEB list.

If you'd like help, an advisor can go through these checks with you on a call for any program you're considering.
